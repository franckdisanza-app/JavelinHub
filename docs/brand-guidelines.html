01 — Foundation

What we'reBuilding

Javelin is a small, deeply technical sport. Throwers train in isolation, coaching is geographically scarce, and useful feedback on a run-up is almost impossible to get. JavelinHub connects throwers to the people who can actually help — session bookings, structured training blocks, and frame-by-frame video review.

The brand should feel like a coach's notebook, not a storefront. Measured, technical, quietly confident. Every design decision should pass one test: would this look out of place written in pencil beside a set of throw distances?

01

Measured

We speak in numbers — meters, degrees, step counts, session lengths. Specificity is what credibility looks like in a technical sport.

02

Unadorned

No hype reels, no stadium pyrotechnics. Progress in throwing comes from repetition and correction. The interface reflects that discipline.

03

On your side

Direct like a good coach — honest about what needs work, never patronising, never selling you a dream instead of a plan.

02 — The Mark

One name,Two weights

The lockup sets JAVELIN at hairline weight against HUB at maximum weight — the visual equivalent of the run-up and the release. Beside it stands the thrower, drawn in one continuous line weight, the javelin held across the body at 34°: the angle the event is decided by.

The figure carries the mark and the shaft carries the angle. Everything else in the system is a rule line or a piece of type.

v1.1 — the mark is two drawings, and that is not a style choice

A javelin rises 0.559 × its own length at 34°. A shaft long enough to read as a javelin therefore needs more width than a square gives it: at full length it wants 52 units of width against 35 of rise, so a square frame holding a full-height athlete caps the shaft at 17.9 units — a stub. The primary mark is landscape, minimum 1.36:1. The square icon is a separate drawing with the shaft deliberately cropped, for favicons and app icons only. Never squash the wide mark into a square; redraw it as the icon or use the icon file.

Clear space

Leave clear space on all four sides equal to the full height of the mark. Nothing crosses it — not navigation, not imagery, not a badge. The javelin tip needs this most: it is the part that reads as broken when something clips it.

Persistent chrome is exempt, and takes half: the app header and footer keep 0.5× the mark height. At full cordon a 30px mark forces a 90px sticky header — 11% of a phone screen, permanently, on every route. The full rule governs the mark placed in something: a page, a card, a share image, print.

Minimum size

The wide mark never goes below 26px tall; under that the figure's limbs merge. Below it, switch to the square icon, which holds down to 16px.

On dark

On Ink, the whole mark flips to Chalk — figure and shaft together, they are one drawing. It stays Sector Blue only on White or Sheet.

v1.1 — accessible name

The lockup is real text, not an image. The bar is decorative and must be hidden from assistive technology; the two words carry the accessible name, which is always the single string JavelinHub — never "Javelin Hub", and never split across two separately labelled elements.

Never
  • Change the javelin's angle. It is 34°, always, everywhere.
  • Set both words at the same weight, or add a space between them.
  • Squash the wide mark into a square — use the icon drawing instead.
  • Fill the figure, add a face, or give it a uniform. It is a line, not a character.
  • Outline, emboss, gradient-fill, or place the lockup over busy footage.
  • Recolor the mark to Turf Green or Foul Red — those colours carry meaning elsewhere.

The ground

Sheet, Chalk and White hold almost everything. This is a video- and text-heavy product; empty space is a feature, not a gap to fill.

The signal

One Sector Blue element per screen in the content area. The mark and persistent chrome do not count against it. If a page body has two blue buttons, one of them isn't the primary action.

The verdicts

Turf Green never initiates anything — it only confirms. Foul Red only reports: a failed payment, a rejected application, a destructive button.

v1.1 — the product footer is light, and Ink chrome is a trap

This document's own footer is Ink. The product's is not, and the difference is arithmetic rather than preference. On Ink, the Sector Blue focus ring measures 2.48:1 — under WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1 floor for a non-text indicator — and Steel meta text measures 2.99:1, under this brand's own 4.5:1. A page footer holds links and meta text; this document's footer holds neither, which is the only reason it gets away with it.

An Ink footer in the app cannot be built without breaking one of two rules already written here: section 06's "the focus ring is never restyled per component", or the contrast floor above. Use Ink for surfaces that carry neither a focus target nor Steel text — the app icon, an inverted lockup, a display panel. Not for interactive chrome.

v1.1 — light only

JavelinHub ships one theme. There is no dark mode: Ink is an accent surface for the footer, the app icon and inverted lockups, not an alternate palette. Any surface that inverts does so because the design calls for it on that specific element, never because of a system preference.

v1.1 — contrast floor

Every enabled foreground/background pair in the product clears 4.5:1. Steel was darkened from #6D746C (4.47:1 on Sheet — a failure, and it carried every label, eyebrow and caption in v1.0) to #5A615A. Steel is the lightest tone that may carry words; nothing lighter is a text colour.

The one carve-out is the one WCAG itself makes: disabled controls are exempt (SC 1.4.3 excludes inactive components). A dimmed button may fall below the floor — it must still be unmistakably inactive, and its state must never be carried by contrast alone.

v1.1 — mono tracking is per role, not one value

Mono tracking varies by what the type is doing, and the specimens above are the spec: form labels +0.14em; buttons and chips +0.1em; section labels and eyebrows +0.16em to +0.22em, which is the widest the system goes and is reserved for type that titles a block rather than sitting inside one; column heads and swatch captions +0.06em. A single flat figure was published in an earlier draft and matched no component on this page. Likewise the mono scale carries a 12px step — what buttons and prices are set in — which that draft's summary line omitted.

v1.1 — family name

The display face is Barlow Condensed, weights 100 / 700 / 900. It replaced Big Shoulders, which two successive problems made unusable.

First, Big Shoulders Display — the family v1.0 specified — has been retired; Google consolidated it into Big Shoulders, and requesting the old name falls back silently to Arial Narrow, which is why v1.0 never rendered as drawn for anyone.

Second, the consolidated name could be fetched but never measured. Next's font catalogue knows only the new name while its font-metrics table still keys the old one, so no metric-matched fallback could be generated and every heading would visibly reflow when the face swapped in. No spelling satisfied both databases. Barlow Condensed is present in both, carries a true 100 and 900 — so the two-weight lockup survives — and is portable in a way the alternatives considered were not: Hammersmith One ships a single weight, and Bahnschrift is a Windows-only system font that cannot be licensed for the web at all.

v1.1 — mono floor is 10px, inputs are 16px

10px is the smallest type in the system; nothing renders below it. The one exception runs the other way: text the user types is always 16px. iOS Safari zooms the viewport whenever a focused field is under 16px, which breaks a 375px layout outright. Labels stay 10px, values are 16px.

05 — Signature Motif

The34° line

The motif is the javelin lifted out of the mark and used on its own — the same angle, the same weight, without the thrower. It is the only decorative element in the system, and it is never purely decorative. It always marks something: a section boundary, a list item, progress through a training block. If it isn't carrying information, remove it.

v1.1 — the line is now derived, not primary

In the first draft the angled bar was the identity, and this section was its main home. Now the thrower is the mark and the line is what remains when the figure is taken away — a detail of the logo rather than a rival to it. That subordination is the point: at small sizes and in running text you want the angle, not a person. Use the figure where the brand is being stated, the line where something is being marked.

v1.1 — units and numbers

Distances are meters to two decimals with a bare m (68.42m). Angles are whole degrees. Money is US dollars, symbol first, two decimals in prose, never 60$ and never USD 60. Session length is minutes (45 MIN). Dates are 18 Aug 2026. Every number in the product is set in Plex Mono.

08 — Revision

What changed inVersion 1.1

v1.0 was written as a design document. v1.1 is what survived contact with an accessible, shipping implementation — nine amendments, each marked in red where it appears above.

v1.0 → v1.1
  1. Steel darkened from #6D746C to #5A615A. The original measured 4.47:1 on Sheet and failed WCAG AA everywhere it was used — which was every label, eyebrow and caption in the system.
  2. Foul Red added (#B3261E). v1.0 had no error colour at all, while its own Voice section specified a failed-payment message. Turf Green confirms; nothing reported failure.
  3. White promoted to a named token. It was already carrying cards, inputs and stages without appearing in the palette.
  4. The one-blue rule scoped to the content area. As written it contradicted itself: blue was reserved for "the mark and the action", which is two on every screen that has a header. The mark and chrome no longer count.
  5. The display face moved to Barlow Condensed. Big Shoulders Display is retired, and its replacement could be fetched but not measured — see section 04. Barlow keeps the two-weight lockup and gets a proper metric-matched fallback.
  6. Input text raised to 16px. The v1.0 mock set fields at 13px, which makes iOS Safari zoom the viewport on focus and breaks a 375px layout. Mono floor set at 10px, and the in-card Book button raised to the 44px target.
  7. System rules made explicit — radius, elevation, rule weight, spacing, touch target, focus. v1.0 stated "corners stay square" in a body sentence and left the rest to inference.
  8. Light-only declared. There is no dark theme; Ink is an accent surface, not an alternate palette.
  9. Units and currency pinned to meters, whole degrees, minutes and US dollars.
  10. The mark is now a thrower, not a bar. The figure carries the identity; the 34° line is what is left when you take the figure away, and it keeps the divider, list-marker and progress roles. The mark exists as two drawings — a landscape primary and a square icon — because at 34° a full-length shaft cannot share a square frame with a full-height athlete.
Corrections applied after the first implementation review
  1. Two contrast figures were miscalculated and are corrected above: Ink on Sheet is 17.71:1, not 15.3; Chalk against Ink is 15.97:1, not 14.3. Both understated the truth, so nothing built against them was at risk — but a document claiming to be what survived an accessible implementation has to have recomputed its own numbers. The other five figures verified exact.
  2. The contrast floor gained the WCAG disabled-control carve-out. As first written it was an unqualified absolute that the product's own disabled Buy control does not meet — correctly, since SC 1.4.3 exempts inactive components.
  3. The mono scale and tracking now match the specimens on this page. The summary line omitted the 12px step that buttons and prices actually use, and flattened three different tracking values into one.
